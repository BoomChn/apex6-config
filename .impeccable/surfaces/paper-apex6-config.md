# Surface: Apex 6 Pro config app (Paper design, v2 "manual")

Scope: the working screens of the personal config app, drawn in Paper. This round covers Buttons and Profiles. Visitor mode: Operate.
Audience and job: the owner changes slot and global settings between play sessions, then writes them to the controller over USB.
Constraints: product truth comes from the v1 Paper spec sheets and PRODUCT.md. Use real Apex 6 Pro photos (MaxGaming press shots, 1600×1280 on white). Do not use a sidebar or card layout; the user rejected both.
Unresolved: which physical keys are M5/M6, and C/Z placement on the photo. Whether Flydigi photos ship in the app is the owner's call.

## Direction contract
THESIS: The screen reads like the "Controls" spread of a console instruction manual. The real controller photo sits at the centre of the page, and leader lines run from each physical button to what it does. It refuses the sidebar + cards + right inspector dashboard.
OWN-WORLD: pure white page (the photo's own ground), ink #111 type and 1px ink leader lines ending in a dot on the button, one cobalt spot ink used like two-colour print for selection, pending edits and the write action, heavy 3px ink rules opening each section, Bai Jamjuree bold headings, tabular mono for values, keycap-square key pickers.
STORY: The owner sees their controller and sees at once what every button does on this slot. They click a button or callout, edit it in the column beside it, and the pending count and the write button tell them nothing has reached the controller yet.
FIRST VIEWPORT: The top bar holds the device and four slot tabs with large numerals, then the sync status and the cobalt write button. Section tabs sit under it. The left 60% is the photo at about 760px with callouts in its margins. The right 40% is the selected button's editor under a heavy rule.
FORM: Game-manual controls page. Position 1 on my list, the pick card. Seed c5f02630.
FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance
